Vancouver,
2098.

The rain-soaked Pacific gateway of humanity's last great age. Not an endless neon metropolis — a network of distinct urban centres around Burrard Inlet, the Fraser River, the Salish Sea and the mountains of the North Shore, joined by rapid transit, autonomous roads, marine routes, aerial corridors and buried infrastructure.

Wet concrete, cedar, old brick, moss, mountain cloud, restrained augmented reality, and warm human light behind rain-streaked glass.

01 // The Golden Age

Yesterday's miracles are
ordinary infrastructure.

Fusion grids keep the lights on. Robots and artificial minds perform much of society's routine labour. The Common Mesh connects identity, commerce, transport, medicine, public services and almost every machine in the city. Gene repair, cloned organs, neural rehabilitation, advanced prosthetics, autonomous vehicles, power armour, orbital industry and publicly licensed heroes are accepted parts of life.

A What works

A heart can be replaced. A continent can be coordinated. Homes exist in orbit. Every citizen is surrounded by information, and armoured responders go into disasters ordinary people could not survive. This is not a facade. It saves lives every day, and it does so at a scale no previous century managed.

B What it costs

Prosperity comes with ownership. Medical subscriptions can bind a person to an employer. Cybernetic limbs can be leased rather than owned. Identity, insurance, employment and contract status determine who receives the miracle and who is told to wait. The society is unequal. It is controlled. It is compromised.

C What is not discussed

Beneath the confident public world lies another Vancouver known only in fragments. Advanced civilisation has produced a plausible explanation for almost everything it cannot account for: sensor error, exotic physics, neurotechnology, engineered mutation, fraudulent media, classified corporate research. Most residents remain certain that any mystery yields to better data.

“Vancouver is not a false paradise waiting to be exposed. It is a sophisticated, unequal, heavily controlled civilisation that genuinely works — and is therefore worth mourning.”

02 // How the City Was Built

Seventy years of emergencies,
none of which were ever removed.

Nothing here was planned. Every crisis since 2030 produced a new authority, standard, database, contractor or security power — and each one outlived the emergency that made it. What you are standing in is the accumulated residue of problems solved in a hurry and then kept.

The foundational decade

Five events between 2030 and 2034 made everything after them possible. None were meant to be permanent. All five are still running.

Periods of the regional record

Eleven periods, 2030 to now. The names are the Index's own; where popular usage differs, the popular name is noted beneath.

WHEN IT BECAME NORMAL

“The city became safer. The city became less understandable. Both sentences are the history of the last seventy years and neither survives the removal of the other.”

03 // The City at a Glance

One region. Many centres.

In civic usage, "Vancouver" means the whole Metro region rather than only the municipal City of Vancouver. The legal municipalities still exist, maintain their own services and political identities, and frequently disagree over jurisdiction. The region no longer revolves around one downtown.

Surrey passed Vancouver during the 2060s and has not been overtaken since. It had the land, multiple urban centres, major hospitals, aerospace and manufacturing campuses, continental freight connections, regional government facilities and the Cloverdale Agritech Belt. It is not a bedroom suburb and has not been one for forty years: it has its own command structure, political priorities, industrial economy and emergency services. Vancouver keeps the internationally recognised name, the financial and governmental districts, the harbour and the orbital gateway — and a daytime population that regularly exceeds Surrey's because of commuters, visitors, port personnel and conference traffic. Both statements are true. Which one is correct depends entirely on whether “Vancouver” means the municipality or the region.

HOW A DISTRICT IS DEFINED

A district is an operational and cultural zone, not necessarily a legal municipality. Residents use neighbourhood names. Municipal systems use service zones. Corporations use property and contract boundaries. Emergency management uses response sectors that may ignore all three.

HOST NATIONS AND TERRITORIES

Indigenous territories and authorities cross several districts. They are governments, communities, land and water authorities, emergency partners, cultural institutions and political powers — not heritage decoration. Tsawwassen First Nation is a distinct government and a major regional partner in land, trade, transport and coastal stewardship.

04 // Living Here

Seeing is not believing.
Provenance is.

Most people go to work, raise families, argue about rent and meet friends at restaurants. What makes 2098 unlike any earlier year is not the technology. It is that survival, safety, medicine, identity and mobility all depend on who recognises your contract — and that the question of whether a thing is true has been replaced by the question of whether it can be proven.

FOUR LEVELS OF TRUST // MESH STANDARD

Synthetic media is perfect. A convincing recording of a head of state confessing to murder can be produced by a teenager's household system in an afternoon. So an image is no longer evidence of anything, and every personal assistant displays a provenance ribbon telling you which of these four you are looking at.

  1. Unverified Entertainment, satire, propaganda, rumour, anonymous leaks, personal expression. It may well be true. Nobody sensible treats it as proof.
  2. Identity-signed A known person or organisation has accepted responsibility for the statement. This proves who published it. It does not prove it is accurate.
  3. Provenance-verified Original capture device, location, time, editing history, witness identities and sensor signatures can all be examined. This is credible news and investigative work.
  4. Evidentiary Multiple independent records, preserved originals, audit trails, physical evidence and documented chain of custody. This supports arrests, court orders, contract enforcement and public emergency action.
The weakness is the opposite of the one people expect. A fake recording will embarrass you. A false entry accepted into the Civic Identity Record will lock you out of your home, freeze your money, cancel your medical authority and persuade the city that you are dead. The most powerful lie in 2098 is not the one everybody watches. It is the one the infrastructure accepts as true.
People who opt out, and people who object

Two groups, frequently confused and frequently overlapping. Both are a visible minority — almost everyone still uses the Mesh, because ordinary life without it is punishingly difficult. But every Lower Mainland municipality is legally required to maintain some manual access services, and every major district has at least one privacy-first cooperative, offline market, independent archive or community that can function without the network.

MANUALISTS The anachronists

They deliberately limit their dependence on the Common Mesh: paper records, mechanical locks, locally stored media, manually operated vehicles, physical currency substitutes, independent radio, appliances that work without continuous authentication.

Nostalgic hobbyists, yes — but also privacy advocates, religious communities, rural cooperatives, disaster-preparedness groups, Host-Nation data-sovereignty advocates, and a very large number of people who have personally survived an identity-system failure and do not intend to survive a second one.

ANARCHISTS The political ones

Most do not reject technology; a great many are excellent technicians. What they oppose is the concentration of authority — in the Civic Identity Record, in corporate contracts, in predictive policing, in proprietary body parts, in employer-controlled augmentation, and in automated systems that quietly decide whether a person may work, travel, receive treatment or enter their own home.

Free-mesh collectives, right-to-repair unions, identity-abolition groups, neighbourhood mutual aid, Grey Workshops, unregistered Underlayer communities.

A CORRECTION What they actually do

Violent cells exist and they are rare. The Index states plainly, because the popular coverage does not: the average anarchist in 2098 is far more likely to repair an undocumented prosthetic, run a community server, expose a predatory contract, or hide somebody from an abusive identity order than to throw a bomb.

The most marginalised parts of this city are also the least dependent on its official explanation of reality. That has been true since 2030 and it is not an accident.

BLUE JUNE // 18 JUNE 2061

The Canucks won the Stanley Cup.

Once. Game seven at Pacific Crown Arena on False Creek, 3–2 in double overtime, four games to three over Toronto. Transit ran free for three days, the harbour was lit blue, and orbital crews sent congratulatory footage down from Freedom Station. The parade started at False Creek, went through downtown, and finished at Surrey Civic Plaza — because by 2061 nobody could pretend the team belonged only to the City of Vancouver.

They have been back to the Final twice since and lost both. Thirty-seven years later the winning goal is still replayed on the False Creek seawall every June the eighteenth, and people born decades after it can tell you exactly where their grandparents were standing.

06 // City Operations

The machinery that has to
stay boring.

Public confidence in 2098 depends on infrastructure remaining ordinary and nearly invisible. These are the systems the region cannot survive a week without, and the authorities that run them.

SYS-01The Common Mesh

Identity, commerce, transit, medicine, public services and almost every machine in the city. Coverage is deliberately uneven in some facilities — total integration would exclude or endanger the people they serve. Every essential service is required to hold a degraded mode that works without it.

SYS-02Power and fusion grid

Fusion generation with hydroelectric backbone from the North Shore watersheds and Tri-Cities corridor. Islanding capability is tested annually: a district must be able to run disconnected from everything else for eleven days.

SYS-03Water and watershed

Capilano, Seymour and Coquitlam watersheds under restricted-land protection, with treatment, reservoirs and desalination support through the Richmond delta. Water authorities and environmental wardens hold field authority that downtown operators routinely underestimate.

SYS-04Flood defence

Intelligent barriers, dikes, pumps and utility galleries across Richmond, Delta, Queensborough, Annacis and the Fraser shore. Managed retreat along the Salish corridor is in its third phase and under public review.

SYS-05Emergency management

NEMA holds regional command with staging at the Abbotsford campus. Municipal fire, medicine and police operate beneath it; corporate continuity contractors operate beside it. Response sectors ignore municipal boundaries by design, which is a recurring source of jurisdictional argument.

SYS-06Health system

The Cambie Medical Axis anchors one of the greatest concentrations of healthcare in the NAA Pacific region, with major capacity at Sapperton, Surrey and the North Shore. Access is governed by identity status, insurance and contract as much as by clinical need.

HAVELOCK IN THE REGION

Havelock Centre in the Burrard Core is the corporate seat and clinical flagship. The company holds the Salish corridor continuity build and its twenty-year maintenance obligation, operates seventeen Pacific Commons hubs, and reaches orbit through Continuity Suite 19-C aboard Northstar Crown. Havelock was incorporated in Vancouver in 2039, during the eleven-day failure of the Fraser Delta dike system, and has never marked that anniversary as a founding.

07 // Places to Visit

What a visitor should
actually see.

A civic guide for residents, visiting personnel and anyone rotating into the region. Everything below is publicly accessible; where a credential or booking is required, it is noted.

V-01WEST END // FREE
Stanley Park and the Seawall

Still green, still wet, still immediately recognisable. Immense cedars, forest paths, beaches and the full seawall circuit, under Host-Nation stewardship and continuous ecological monitoring. Utility and emergency installations are present and carefully concealed; the park reads as forest because keeping it that way is a maintained decision.

Best in rain. Most residents will tell you this and they are right.
V-02GASTOWN // AR OVERLAY
The Gastown Memory Quarter

Old brick, narrow alleys, rail history, steam infrastructure and licensed augmented reconstructions of a vanished Vancouver. Stand on a corner and watch a building that burned in 1886 resolve around you. Authenticity here is both civic culture and commercial product — the archive can rebuild a lost street, but somebody still decides which version becomes the official one.

Overlay access is free. The unlicensed community reconstructions are more interesting.
ANOMALY // FILED, NOT ACTIONED

A figure in a red-and-white striped shirt stands beside the Steam Clock. He is visible only on the approved 1980s heritage channel. His stripes are an augmented-reality skin and his identity settings classify him as a decorative cultural asset, which is why the civic cameras have been ignoring him since 2094. Four requests to remove him have been filed. All four were closed on the ground that there is nothing there to remove.

V-03OLYMPIC BASIN // TICKETED
Pacific Crown Arena

The region's mass-event and licensed-hero showcase venue: regulated professional sport, enhanced leagues, hero exhibitions, concerts, public ceremonies and emergency recruitment events. The Canucks' 2061 championship — remembered regionally as Blue June — remains embedded in the public projections and the seawall memorials outside.

Blue June displays run continuously outside the arena, no ticket required.
V-04RICHMOND // FREE
Steveston

A working maritime village that never surrendered its identity to the delta grid around it. Boatyards, fisheries research, marine biotechnology and a public waterfront where the fleet still lands. The best place in the region to watch heavy marine automation and small independent operators share a dock without either pretending the other does not exist.

Reachable by marine transit from False Creek in about forty minutes.
V-05MOUNTAIN EDGE // SEASONAL
Grouse, Cypress and the Sea-to-Sky

Cable systems, mountain roads, forest preserve and weather that can isolate a party within minutes. The view across the inlet at dusk is the single best argument for the city that anyone has ever made. Search-and-rescue bases operate year-round, and their advice about conditions is not advisory.

Check the mountain weather plan before departure. Every year somebody does not.
V-06NORTH SHORE // FREE
Lower Lonsdale Shipyards

Public waterfront built directly into a live marine-industrial site: ferry traffic, shipyards, marine robotics and covered markets, with the cranes working overhead. One of the few places where 2098 lets the public stand inside heavy industry rather than behind a viewing wall.

SeaBus from Harbour Crown, twelve minutes, runs continuously.
V-07WESTERN PENINSULA // FREE
Pacific Spirit and Jericho

Protected forest, ecological laboratory, watershed buffer and public refuge on the western peninsula, with the Jericho coastal-research commons alongside. Jointly managed by public institutions, Host-Nation authorities, universities and emergency agencies — an unusually functional example of shared authority.

Mesh coverage inside the forest is patchy. This is not an accident and not a fault.
V-08CLOVERDALE // SEASONAL
Cloverdale Fairgrounds

Fair, rodeo, equestrian grounds and agricultural show, in the middle of the western centre of a continental food-security system. Seed vaults and livestock genetics laboratories sit within sight of the midway. Both are the same civic project, which is the point.

Public research tours run through the summer. Book ahead; they fill.
V-09SOUTH VANCOUVER // FREE
Fraser Delta Memorial Gardens

Eleven gardens for the eleven days the dike system failed in 2039. Names are read across eleven days each August. Free entry, always, for Havelock personnel and their families — a standing commitment the company has never once described as a benefit.

Quiet on any ordinary day. Bring nothing; leave nothing.
V-10RICHMOND // OBSERVATION DECK
YVR Crown and the Northstar Corridor

The public observation levels at YVR Crown look directly onto spaceplane operations and the Northstar aerospace corridor. For most residents this is as close as orbit gets: watching other people's cargo and other people's medical transfers leave the atmosphere on schedule.

Departure windows are published on the civic mesh. Weather holds are common.
08 // Getting Around

Four networks, one credential.

Rapid transit

The spine of the region, linking Vancouver to Burnaby, New Westminster, Surrey and the Tri-Cities. Transit exchanges are civic buildings in their own right — Surrey Central, Metrotown, Lougheed and Oakridge each function as a shelter and services complex as much as a station.

Marine routes

SeaBus across the inlet, False Creek ferries, marine taxis, and scheduled service to Steveston and the coastal gate. Water makes several districts unusually permeable, and unusually easy to isolate when a crossing fails.

Aerial corridors

Autonomous vehicle lanes and rooftop pads, tightly controlled over the Core and Harbour Crown. Mountain approaches contend with downdrafts, rotor wash and cloud layers that change faster than automated routing expects.

Autonomous roads

Regional road network under coordinated routing, with bridges as the recurring constraint. A single damaged crossing on the North Shore, the Fraser or the Eastern Gate can isolate large populations within the hour.

A NOTE ON THE UNDERLAYER

Beneath all four networks lies the accumulated hidden geography of a century of rebuilding: live service corridors, legacy infrastructure, drowned pre-seawall streets, and spaces that were removed from the record deliberately. Power authorities know their galleries. Transit knows its bores. Port security knows its freight routes. No complete map exists, because such a map would bypass too many security and property systems at once.

The Underlayer is not a tourist destination. It contains automatic flood cycles, bad air, live power, unstable concrete and doors that may be opened remotely by systems several kilometres away.
